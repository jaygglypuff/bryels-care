One order goes through 16 steps, from **Ask to buy** to **Invoice**. The **How work flows** screen shows all of them on one page. Open it from **Make → How work flows**, or type "how work flows" in **Search**.

## What each row shows

1. **The step**, for example **Put away** or **Second check of the box**.
2. **Who does it**, for example **Warehouse** or **QA (two people)**.
3. **How many wait there now**, for example **2 lots OK, waiting for a shelf**.
4. **How old the oldest one is**, for example **3 days**.
5. **One button** to the screen where that person does the step.

## The 16 steps

1. Ask to buy
2. Order from the supplier
3. Delivery coming
4. Receive and label
5. QA check of deliveries
6. Put away
7. Customer orders
8. Make it
9. Get materials · weigh · steps · pack · finish
10. QA OK of batches
11. Set aside
12. Get from shelf
13. Second check of the box
14. Send out
15. Proof of delivery
16. Invoice

## Red rows

1. A row is **red** when something waits there and **nobody was told** about it. No message was sent to the person who does the step.
2. Tap the red number. The list shows each thing with **Nobody told**.
3. Tell the person who does the step, or open the thing yourself if it is your job.

## Short, or coming?

1. A customer order is **short: it needs making** only when the stock for it is not there and is not coming.
2. Stock that is **coming** counts: a batch **waiting for QA**, a batch **being made** in an approved production order, and a production order made for this order that waits for approval. A production order made for another customer order does not count.
3. Such an order shows as **order waiting for stock that is coming**, for example "Isla Spa Supplies · coming: 48 waiting for QA". It is never red: nobody has to act, the stock is on its way.
4. The planner's Home ("Customer orders short") and the shortage card on the customer order use the same rule.

## See what waits

1. Tap a number, for example **6 lots waiting for the QA check**.
2. You see each thing, the oldest first, with how long it waits.
3. Tap one to open it.

## What you see depends on your job

1. You see only the steps your job may see. Purchasing sees buying and deliveries. The warehouse sees receiving, putting away and sending out.
2. You see only your own site. Change the site at the top to see another one.

## Your Home

1. **Purchasing** sees **Your buying work** on Home: requests to order, purchase orders to send, deliveries late and due today.
2. **The warehouse** sees **Orders to send out** on Home: to get from the shelf, to check (a different person), to send out, and proof of delivery missing.
3. The numbers on Home are the same as on How work flows.
