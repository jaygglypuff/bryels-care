Most screens use everyday words. Six industry words stay, because they are on the paper and the labels too.

## The six words we keep

- **Lot**: one delivery (or one batch) of something, with its own expiry date. Every sack or box label shows its lot number.
- **Batch**: one round of making a product, for example one pot of soap.
- **Quality check**: before anything is used or sold, the QA person checks it and says OK or not OK.
- **Batch record (BMR)**: the paper that shows how a batch was made: who weighed what, readings and checks.
- **INCI**: the official name of an ingredient that goes on the product label.
- **Version**: a recipe or a list of steps can change over time. Each change is a new version; old batches keep the version they used.

## Plain words on the screens

- **OK to use** = released by QA. **Not OK – don't use** = rejected. **Stopped by QA** = on hold.
- **Waiting for QA check** = not checked yet (quarantine).
- **Production order** = the plan to make a product: which product, how many, by when.
- **Save for good** = post: after this it cannot be changed, only corrected with a new record.
- **To approve** = waits for someone's OK (approval).
- **History of changes** = the audit log: who changed what, when and why.
- **Needs the owner's OK** = a restricted action that only the owner may do or allow.
- **Job (role)** = what a person may do in the app: Owner, Site manager, Machine operator, Warehouse, QA, Sales, Finance.
- **Technical name** = the code a programmer uses; you never need to type it.
- **Problem report** = deviation. **Fix-and-prevent plan** = CAPA. **Who received it** = recall trace.
